# Figure 1 caption

**Figure 1. Four axes of seasonal thermal structure, where each acts, and
two real cue regimes.** Panels A and B are schematic, built from idealized
annual sinusoids with deliberately exaggerated changes: the real observed
changes are far too small to see at annual-cycle scale (the largest
regional phase shift measured, 6.07 days, is 1.7% of a 365-day axis), so
plotting them at true scale would show curves that appear unchanged.
Every schematic sub-panel carries the true observed magnitude range from
Figure 2 as a caption, so the exaggeration is declared rather than
implied. Panel C uses real site climatologies, because it contrasts two
*reefs* rather than two *periods*, and that contrast (amplitude 0.85 vs.
2.61 degC; interannual timing spread 17.7 vs. 6.8 days) is large enough to
show directly. Historical/reference curves are black throughout; altered
curves are blue/orange (never used to mean a region, unlike Figure 2's
use of colour). Grey shading around a curve is a fixed-width reaction-norm
envelope (physiological tolerance, illustrative); the grey dashed
horizontal line is a bleaching threshold.

**(A1) Mean.** Historical curve (black) and a uniformly warmed curve
(orange, same shape), narrowing the gap to the bleaching threshold
("margin"). The only sub-panel with a single-headed arrow: mean warming
is the one axis moving in a single direction everywhere in the data.
*Observed: +0.08 to +0.33 degC decade⁻¹ pooled regional trend, all 9
poolable regions positive (Table S4).*

**(A2) Amplitude.** Historical curve plus one larger-amplitude and one
smaller-amplitude alternative, same mean. Double-headed arrow (bidirectional
by construction). Labels give each curve's "ramp" -- days spent within
0.5 degC of the mean on the rising limb -- which shortens under larger
amplitude and lengthens under smaller amplitude (the steeper or shallower
slope through the mean-crossing); this is the mechanism panel B labels
"acclimation ramp." *Observed: -0.03 to +0.08 degC decade⁻¹, naive
full-record trend, regionally divergent -- 4 regions increased
significantly, 1 decreased (Table S5).*

**(A3) Phase.** Historical curve plus one earlier- and one later-peaking
alternative (double-headed arrow; both directions shown, matching the
data, where 3 regions shifted later and 1 earlier). A fixed vertical line
marks the June solstice, the photoperiod reference against which lag to
each curve's peak is given (23/48/78 days). Because the solstice cannot
move, a change in a region's thermal phase is by construction an equal
change in its separation from the photoperiod reference -- the
decoupling mechanism this panel exists to show. *Observed: -2.9 to +6.1
days regional pooled phase shift, first-vs-last-15-year, regionally
divergent (Table S6).*

**(A4) Cue reliability.** Eight synthetic year-curves at one idealized
site: peak timing jittered with a narrow SD, peak height (magnitude)
jittered with a much wider one, illustrating that timing is predictable
while magnitude is only weakly so -- built from scratch, not adapted from
any intra-seasonal-variance framing (no evidence supports that mechanism;
see Section 5 exclusions below). Both spreads are grounded in real values
(median circular SD across 81 sites, 8.85 days; median interannual SD of
detrended annual peak SST across 7 real sites, 0.40 degC) and then
exaggerated 3x for legibility with only 8 sample curves -- reported
explicitly, not left implicit. *Observed: pooled rho=0.22 (Table S9/S10),
~5% of variance in peak magnitude (r²), i.e. most of that real 0.40 degC
spread is not explained by any early-season cue.*

**(B) Where each axis acts.** One larger schematic curve (same
construction as A1-A3's historical curve), annotated with leader lines at
four points: **safety margin** (mean; gap between seasonal maximum and
the bleaching threshold), **acclimation ramp** (amplitude; the rising
limb from seasonal minimum to maximum), **cue-response alignment**
(phase; horizontal offset between the photoperiod reference and the
thermal maximum), and **preparation calibration** (cue reliability;
vertical uncertainty in peak height given the cue). An annotated single
curve was sufficient at this panel's size; the compact-table fallback
considered in the task brief was not needed.

**(C) Two real cue regimes**, the same two sites used in Figure 2 panel
D's inset (South_Central_Pacific_Polynesia_03, low amplitude;
Western_Indian_Ocean_01, high amplitude), so a reader encountering them
in both figures connects the schematic to the measurement. For each site:
raw daily climatology (thin, light trace, 44-year mean per calendar day)
under a 15-day centred moving-average smooth (heavy line) -- the raw
trace carries the low-amplitude site's rougher seasonal texture, part of
what makes the regime distinction concrete. Horizontal double-headed
arrow at each curve's peak: that site's own real interannual
peak-timing spread (+/- 1 circular SD), the element carrying this panel's
claim. A1 and circular SD annotated directly, in the curve's own colour.
The 1.13 degC amplitude breakpoint (Figure 2 panel D) is deliberately not
printed here: that is the quantitative result, and belongs to Figure 2;
this panel is its qualitative, illustrated counterpart.

Panel letters bold, upper left. Colourblind-safe palette (Okabe-Ito)
throughout, matched to Figure 2 (same blue/orange/green hex values). All
four panel-A observed-magnitude figures were checked against Figure 2's
own Supplementary Tables (S4-S6, S9-S10) before drawing, not transcribed
from a prompt or from memory.

**What does not appear**, per the exclusions established for this figure
(none of the two conceptual figures it replaces are reproduced or
adapted): phase advance is not shown as *the* phase change (A3 is
bidirectional); intra-seasonal variance is not used as a cue-degradation
mechanism (A4 is built from scratch around real magnitude-vs-timing
spread, not variance inflation); amplitude compression is not the sole
amplitude consequence (A2 is bidirectional); cue reliability is not shown
declining over time (A4 is a single-site, multi-year snapshot, not a
trend); and there are no coral icons, no Healthy/Pale/Bleached
progression, and no resilience Venn diagram.
